Methodology

How a fixture becomes a probability. Every step is deterministic and reproducible from the committed scripts and the public sources listed on the source register.

1. Fixture inventory

We read the same public fixtures feed that powers play.hotspawn.com/lol-pickems: GET /api/v1/fixtures?sport_alias=lol&tenantId=1. The current snapshot contains 264 fixtures with status scheduled and 1102 completed fixtures used as the results reference. Team names, competition, tier, start time and fixture id are taken verbatim; nothing is renamed. Where the server-rendered lobby exposes a match permalink we attach it, otherwise the row links the lobby.

2. Market prices (primary signal)

For each fixture we look for an external, timestamped price:

  • Kalshi — CFTC-regulated event exchange, series KXLOLGAME. We take the mid-point of the yes bid/ask for both outcomes and normalise the pair to 100%.
  • Pinnacle — licensed bookmaker, esports moneyline. American prices are converted to decimal, implied probabilities are computed as 1/decimal, then normalised (proportional de-vig): p_i = (1/o_i) / Σ(1/o_j).
  • Polymarket — prediction market, “Match Winner” market only; outcome prices are normalised.

A quote is attached only when both team names match after normalisation and the quote starts within 36 hours of the Hotspawn fixture. No fuzzy matching, no manual overrides.

When several venues quote the same match we blend them: p_market = Σ(w·p) / Σw with w = 0.5 Pinnacle, 0.4 Kalshi, 0.3 Polymarket.

3. Results model (fallback for unpriced leagues)

A standard Elo run over completed fixtures, ordered by date:

expected = 1 / (1 + 10^((R_b - R_a)/400))
R' = R + K · recency · (score - expected)
K = 32 (S) · 28 (A) · 24 (B) · 20 (C) · 16 (D / unknown)
recency = 0.5 ^ (age_in_days / 180)

Ratings start at 1500. Because a team with two matches must not look as certain as a team with twenty, ratings are shrunk toward the mean before conversion: R_shrunk = 1500 + (R - 1500) · n/(n+8).

Series length: if Hotspawn publishes best_of, the single-game probability is expanded to a series probability; otherwise the fixture is treated as a single game.

Which ratings are used. Two independent Elo pools are maintained: one from the Leaguepedia CargoExport match archive (preferred, independent of Hotspawn) and one from completed Hotspawn fixtures (fallback for competitions Leaguepedia does not carry). A match uses the Leaguepedia pool when both teams have at least three completed matches there, otherwise the Hotspawn pool, otherwise it is unresolved. The pool used is shown on every pick row.

Minimum evidence. A model pick is only published when both teams have at least 3 completed fixtures in the reference set. Otherwise the match is unresolved.

Probabilities are floored at 2% and capped at 98%. Fair odds are 1/p; they are our line, not a quote copied from a book.

5. Known limitations (also on the review page)

  • Bookmaker coverage of national third divisions, regional cups and promotion brackets is effectively zero; those rows cannot have a market-derived line.
  • Elo is only as good as the results reference. If a team changed roster, the rating lags reality.
  • Cross-league comparisons are weak by construction: a Tunisian and a German third-division team never share opponents. Ratings are interpretable inside a competition, which is where they are used.
  • Some Hotspawn fixtures stay scheduled long after their start time. They are excluded and flagged rather than priced.
  • Markets move. Every quote carries its retrieval time; a row is only as fresh as the last pipeline run (every 6 hours).

6. What we deliberately do not do

  • We do not use Hotspawn’s crowd “% picked”, tiers or points as a probability input.
  • We do not infer a missing result, roster or price.
  • We do not carry a value forward from a previous run without a timestamp.

Independent line generation. Fixture list from Hotspawn Play. Market prices from public, citable exchange/bookmaker APIs. Hotspawn's crowd “% picked” is never used to set a probability.
